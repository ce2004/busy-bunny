title: A Voice by the Pond

The reeds by the pond are cool and springy under {name}'s paws. From somewhere in the middle comes a big, round voice singing scales. Do, re, mi, and then a very confident fa that wobbles like jelly.

It is Fennel the frog, standing on a lily pad with one webbed hand pressed to his chest. He is small and green and shiny, and he bows when he sees {name}. "An audience!" he says. "At last, an audience of one. That is my favorite size."

{name} sits on a warm rock and listens to the rest of the song. When it ends, {name} claps so hard it nearly tips into the water. Fennel says that is the best review he has ever had.

title: The Humming Lesson

Fennel's paw is cool and a little damp when he shakes hands. "Today," he says, "we hum." He explains that every great singer starts with a hum, because a hum tickles your nose from the inside.

{name} tries. The hum comes out like a sleepy bee. Fennel nods very seriously. "Good. Now lower." {name} hums lower, and its whole tummy buzzes. It giggles, and the giggle ruins the hum, and Fennel laughs his big croaky laugh.

They hum together until the dragonflies come to see what the noise is. By the end, {name} can hold one long, steady note. Fennel says it is the note of someone who is going to be very good at this, and {name} hops home humming all the way.

title: Choir Practice

The village hall smells of wood polish and warm tea. Chairs scrape, someone sneezes, and Dot the duck quacks a quick hello. This is choir practice, and Fennel has saved {name} a seat in the front row.

Fennel taps a little stick on his music stand. Tap, tap. Everyone goes quiet. Then he lifts his arms like a grand conductor, and the whole room begins to sing a song about the moon.

{name} does not know all the words yet, so it hums the parts it does not know. Nobody minds. Rocco sings too loud, Hazel sings very softly, and Mrs. Teasel sings one beat behind everyone, always. Fennel says that is what makes it a choir and not a machine.

The talent show is in one week, and Fennel is hosting, as always. He paces across his kitchen floor, his feet going pat, pat, pat on the tiles. "What if my opening joke is not funny?" he asks.

{name} offers him a carrot to calm down. Fennel does not eat carrots, but he holds it like a microphone, and that helps. He tells the joke to {name}. It is about a frog who wanted to be a prince but liked being a frog too much.

{name} laughs, a real laugh, the kind that squeaks at the end. Fennel's throat puffs out with pride. "You see?" he says. "Even hosts need an audience of one sometimes." He tucks the carrot behind his ear for luck.

title: Showtime

The village green is full of soft blankets, rustling snack bags, and the warm buzz of everyone talking at once. Lanterns hang from the trees and sway a little in the breeze.

Fennel steps onto the stage in his purple cape. He tells his joke, and the whole crowd laughs, and Rocco laughs loudest of all. Then the acts begin. Pip juggles acorns. Bramble plays a tune on his wrenches. Gerald the snail crosses the stage very slowly, and everyone cheers him on the whole way.

{name} sits on the front blanket with a cup of warm cider between its paws. At the end, Fennel bows so low his crown falls off. He catches it, puts it back on, and winks right at {name}.

title: A Rainy Rehearsal

Rain drums on the roof of Fennel's cottage, steady and soft. Inside, it is warm and smells like peppermint tea. Fennel says rainy days are perfect for practicing, because the rain is like a drummer who never gets tired.

He teaches {name} a song with clapping in it. Clap, clap, pause, clap. {name} gets the pause wrong every time and claps right into the quiet part. Fennel decides that this is now a new version of the song, and it is better.

They sing it again and again, until the rain slows down to a drip, drip, drip. Fennel says the rain clapped along with them in the end. {name} is pretty sure that is true.

title: The Lost High Note

One morning Fennel arrives at the burrow with a scarf wrapped three times around his neck. His voice is a tiny croak. "I have misplaced my high note," he whispers dramatically. "It is surely somewhere in the village."

{name} makes him honey tea, warm and sticky and sweet. They sit by the fire, and the logs pop and hiss. Fennel sips and sighs and tells long stories in his whisper voice, which makes every story sound like a secret.

By the afternoon, the tea has done its job. Fennel hums, then sings a little, then sings a lot. The high note comes back, round and bright. "It was in the honey all along," he says, and gives {name} a very grateful bow.
