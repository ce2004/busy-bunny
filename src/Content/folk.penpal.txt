Dear {name},

Hello from Willowmere, over the hills! My name is Juniper, and I am a bunny too. I live in a little burrow right beside the river. At night, I can hear the water burbling past my window, soft and steady, like someone humming in the next room.

My favorite thing to do is dip my toes in the river when the sun is warm. The stones on the bottom are smooth and round, and they tickle.

I would love to be pen pals. What is your burrow like? Do you have a favorite carrot? I like the small sweet ones best.

Your new friend,
Juniper

Dear {name},

Thank you for being my pen pal! I read your letter on the riverbank with my paws in the cool grass.

I must tell you about the Willowmere bakery. It only bakes on Thursdays. Nobody knows why. The baker, an old hare named Mr. Thistle, just says Thursday is the day his oven likes best. So every Thursday the whole village smells like warm bread, and everyone lines up with baskets.

On the other days, we eat Thursday bread. It is still very good.

Does your village have a bakery? What do they make?

With crumbs on my whiskers,
Juniper

Dear {name},

Today I visited the windmill at the top of our hill. If you put your paw on the wooden door, you can feel it shiver gently as the sails turn. Inside, it creaks and groans in a friendly way, like an old chair settling.

The miller lets me sit on a flour sack and listen. Whoosh, creak, whoosh, creak. It is the most peaceful sound in Willowmere.

I came home dusted in flour, and my mother said I looked like a little snow bunny.

What is the most peaceful sound where you live?

Your friend,
Juniper

Dear {name},

It rained all week in Willowmere, so I stayed in and learned to make carrot soup. It is thick and warm and you have to stir it slowly with a wooden spoon. The spoon goes tock, tock against the side of the pot.

I made too much, of course. I gave some to my neighbors, a family of mice, and they made me a tiny thank you card out of a leaf.

The river got very full and loud from the rain. It sounded like it was laughing.

What do you like to cook? Or do you like eating best? I like eating best.

Your friend,
Juniper

Dear {name},

Big news! Thursday came, and Mr. Thistle baked a giant loaf of bread shaped like a fish. Nobody knows why. He said the oven wanted to try something new. It was warm and crusty and fed the whole village.

I ate the tail. It was the crunchiest part.

Then I had a nap on the bridge over the river. The boards were sun-warm and smooth. A duck floated by and quacked at me, very politely.

Tell me about your neighbors. Are any of them as funny as Mr. Thistle?

With a full tummy,
Juniper

I practiced on the riverbank all afternoon. A frog answered me. I do not know what I said, but he seemed happy about it.

Your letters make my week so cozy. I keep them in a little wooden box by my bed. The lid is smooth and has a carrot carved on it.

Can you whistle? Or do you squeak too?

Dear {name},

Willowmere had a festival this week for the windmill's birthday. It is one hundred years old! We tied ribbons to the sails, and when they turned, the ribbons fluttered and snapped in the wind like little flags.

Mr. Thistle baked a cake, even though it was a Tuesday. Everyone gasped. He said birthdays are allowed to break the rules.

There was dancing on the green, and I danced so much my ears flopped every which way.

Does your village have festivals? What is the best one?

Your friend,
Juniper

We sing songs and tell stories and eat Thursday bread toasted on long forks. The bread gets crunchy on the outside and stays soft inside.

Last night I fell asleep on a flour sack, and someone covered me with a blanket. I woke up warm and dusty and very happy.

What do you do on winter evenings?

She says it is the warmest scarf she has ever worn, lumps and all. I think that is the nicest thing a scarf can be.

The river froze at the edges this week. If you tap the ice with your paw, it makes a tiny tink sound, like a bell.

Have you ever heard ice sing? It is very sweet.

It is the last letter of the season, but only the season. I will keep writing, I promise, for as long as you like.

Spring is here in Willowmere. The river is singing again, the windmill is turning, and the bakery smells warm, because it is Thursday. Mr. Thistle did not bake on Friday after all. He said the oven needed a rest. Ovens are like bunnies that way.

Thank you for all your letters. They made my whole winter cozy. I keep every one in my carrot box.

Your friend, always,
Juniper
