A soft, sleepy hum drifts from the reeds where Dot sits on her nest. The eggs tucked under her feel warm as fresh bread rolls. She fluffs up like a small brown cushion and refuses to move for anyone.

Tap. Tap tap. A tiny knock sounds from inside one shell, and Dot quacks back as if answering the door. The whole pond goes very quiet to listen.

Peep! Peep peep! Five damp, fuzzy ducklings tumble out of their shells and into the morning. They are softer than dandelion fluff and already very loud about it.

Little webbed feet patter on the mud, pat pat pat, then a tiny bump as someone sits down by surprise. The ducklings wobble after Dot in a crooked line, peeping directions to each other.

Splash, then a delighted chorus of peeps. The ducklings meet the water for the first time and bob like corks. Dot paddles in slow circles, quacking that they are doing beautifully.

Kick, kick, glide, quacks Dot, giving her first proper swimming lesson. Cool water swirls around the reeds as five small tails wiggle in time. One duckling swims backwards the whole way and looks very proud anyway.

Plip, plop, bottoms up. The ducklings are learning to dabble, tipping headfirst into the water with only their tails poking out. They pop back up sputtering duckweed and peeping about treasure.

This week the peeps sound different, squeaky little calls that crack into a tiny quack. Their down feels thicker now, like a warm wool sock. Dot says this is perfectly normal and hides a smile.

Under the soft fuzz, the first real feathers are coming in, smooth and a little prickly at the tips. The ducklings preen with busy clicks of their bills. They look a bit patchy, like a quilt still being sewn.

Flap flap flap, like someone shaking out a small rug. The ducklings practice beating their wings on the bank without ever leaving the ground. Gerald the snail watches and declares it a very windy afternoon.

A whoosh, a splash, and a cheer of quacks. The bravest duckling lifts off for its first short flight, skimming just above the water before landing with a happy splat. Its siblings line up to try next.

All five ducklings now flutter across the pond in short, buzzy hops. The air hums with wings and proud quacks. Otis hoots from the post office roof that he has never seen finer flying, and he has seen a lot.

Full, grown-up quacks ring across the pond at sunrise now, a busy honking chatter. Their feathers feel sleek and slip under a fingertip like silk. Dot can barely keep up with all their plans.

The young ducks paddle off to explore the far side of the pond on their own. Their wakes roll back to the shore in gentle lapping ripples. They return every evening with long stories about a very interesting stick.

Slap, slap, slap go webbed feet on the village green as the ducks practice marching in a line. Rocco offers to crow the beat for them. They politely decline and quack it themselves.

Quack, quack, quack, quack, quack! The grown ducklings proudly lead their very own little parade around the pond, with Dot waddling happily at the back. The whole village claps along, and you get the best spot by the reeds.
