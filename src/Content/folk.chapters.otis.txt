title: The Morning Yawn

The post office counter is smooth wood, worn soft by a thousand parcels. It smells of paper and string. Behind it, something enormous and fluffy lets out the longest yawn you have ever heard. "Hooooo."

"Good morning," says Otis, very slowly. "Or good night. For me it is bedtime." His feathers brush your hand, soft as a fluffed pillow. One feather on top of his head sticks straight up.

{name} reaches over and pats it down. It springs right back up. {name} pats it again. Boing. Otis watches with one sleepy eye. "That feather has been doing that since I was an owlet," he says. "I have made my peace with it. You may keep trying, if it makes you happy."

title: The Letter with No Address

This week Otis slides an envelope across the counter. It is crinkly and a bit lumpy, and it smells strongly of carrots.

"No address," says Otis. "No name. Just a carrot drawing and a great deal of enthusiasm." He blinks slowly. "I have sorted mail for forty winters. I believe this is for you."

{name} opens it with a happy rip. Inside is a crumbly oat biscuit and a card from Dot the duck that just says, in wobbly letters, Hello friend. {name} shares the biscuit with you, then holds the card up to Otis.

"A mystery solved," Otis says, settling his feathers. "That is the best part of the job. That, and naps."

title: Learning to Sort

Otis lets you behind the counter today. The sorting shelf is a wall of little wooden cubbies, smooth inside, each one with a raised letter you can feel with your fingertip.

"Hazel's go here," he says. Soft rustle. "Pip's go here, and there will be many." Rustle rustle rustle. "Rocco gets mostly fan mail. From Rocco."

{name} takes this job very seriously. It sorts one letter, then announces it loudly. "Library!" Another. "Stable!" A third. "Carrot!" Otis tilts his head. "There is no carrot cubby," he says. {name} points. There is now a carrot cubby, labeled in crayon. Otis considers it for a long moment, then slowly nods. "Fine," he says. "Every post office needs one."

title: A Riddle in the Mail

A small folded note waits in your cubby this week. The paper is thick and soft, and when you unfold it, it crackles gently.

"Otis left it at dawn," says {name}, bouncing. "It is a riddle. I have already guessed eleven wrong answers."

Otis peeks out from his sleeping perch with one eye open. "Read it slowly," he says. His dry voice is muffled by feathers. The riddle says: I have a door but no walls, a mouth but no teeth, and I eat only letters. What am I?

{name} thinks very hard. Its nose wiggles. "A very polite monster," it guesses. Otis hoots with laughter, a sound like a soft drum. "The mailbox," he says. "But I like your answer better."

title: The Night Shift

Tonight {name} is helping Otis with the night shift. The post office is cool and dim, and the only sounds are the tick of the wall clock and the soft rustle of envelopes.

"Sorting at night is peaceful," says Otis. "The letters are sleepy too. They do not argue."

{name} lasts very bravely until about half past nine. Then it climbs into an empty mail sack, which is soft canvas and smells like string, and curls up with a sigh. Soon there is a tiny snore.

Otis carefully drapes his scarf over the sack. It is woolly and warm. "Special delivery," he whispers to you. "One bunny, very tired, handle with care." He keeps sorting by moonlight, humming softly so it can sleep.

===
title: The Pen Pal

There is a parcel this week that smells of salt and sea breeze. The paper is a little damp at the corners, and it crinkles like seaweed.

"From Marlow," Otis explains. "The gray seal who keeps the lighthouse. Marlow has been writing to me for years. Mostly about the weather. Sometimes about fish."

Inside is a smooth, cool pebble with a hole worn right through it, and a letter that Otis reads aloud in his slow dry voice. Dear Otis. The sea is wet again this week. Please say hello to your new friends. I hear they are very nice.

{name} gasps. "Marlow knows about us!" Otis nods. "I may have mentioned you," he says. "Once or twice. Or every letter."

title: A New Perch

Clank, clank, hummm. Bramble arrives at the post office this week carrying something big. You run your hand over it. It is a perch made of smooth curved wood, wrapped in soft fleece where an owl might rest.

"For Otis," Bramble rumbles. "His old one squeaked."

Otis tries it out. He settles, fluffs, and lets out a long, deep hoo of pure contentment. His one wild feather pops up happily. "This," he says, "is the finest perch I have ever sat upon. I may never leave."

{name} climbs up beside him to test it too. It is very comfy. Within a minute, both of them are dozing. Bramble chuckles and tiptoes out, his toolbox clanking as quietly as a toolbox can.

title: The Thank You Box

Otis brings out a wooden box this week. It is heavy, smooth, and rattles softly when he sets it down. "I have never shown anybody this," he says.

He lifts the lid. Inside are notes. Hundreds of them. Some crinkly, some soft, some folded into tiny squares. "Thank you notes," he says. "From everyone in the village. Every one I ever got. I keep them all."

He picks one out. It is on carrot colored paper, written in crayon. Dear Otis, thank you for the carrot cubby. Love, {name}. {name} hides its face in its paws with delight. "You kept it," it squeaks. Otis puffs up his feathers. "Of course," he says. "It is one of my favorites."

===
title: Letters for Everyone

This week Otis has an idea, and he looks very awake about it, which is unusual. "Let us write a letter," he says, "to every single neighbor. Just to say hello."

He gives you smooth cards and a pencil that is soft and easy to hold. The post office fills with quiet scratching sounds. {name} writes to Gerald the snail. It takes a while, because {name} insists on explaining every word to Gerald first.

Otis writes the most. His notes are short and dry and funny. Dear Pip, everyone knows. Dear Rocco, it was loud, but nice. Dear Clover, bring apples.

At the end there is a big warm pile of letters. "Tonight," says Otis, "I deliver them all. It will be the happiest night shift of my life."

title: A Stamp of Your Own

The last week of the season, Otis waits at the counter wide awake, holding something behind his wing. He hands it over. It is a tiny rubber stamp with a smooth wooden handle. When you press your finger on the rubber, you feel a little raised shape. Two long ears, a round body, and a small heart beside it.

"An official post office stamp," says Otis. "For you and {name}. Any letter you send, I will deliver first. Even before Rocco's fan mail."

{name} stamps everything it can reach. Thump, thump, thump. The counter, a card, its own paw. Otis lets out a slow, happy hoo. "Write to me anytime," he says. "Day or night. I will always be glad to see your stamp."
