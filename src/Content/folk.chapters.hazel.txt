title: The Comfy Chair

The library door is heavy and cool under your paw, and inside the air is warm and smells like old paper and cinnamon. A rug as soft as moss squishes underfoot. Somewhere a page turns with a tiny whisper.

"Welcome," says Hazel, in her quietest library voice. Her prickles brush your hand, soft as a hairbrush. She leads you to a big armchair with a dip in the middle, just the right size.

{name} climbs in first, wiggles, and announces in a very loud whisper that this is the best chair in the history of chairs. Hazel's spectacles slide down her nose as she smiles. "Then it is yours," she says. "Every week. I will keep it warm."

title: The Notebook

A small notebook sits on the arm of your comfy chair this week. Its cover is soft leather, worn smooth at the corners, and it is tied with a ribbon that feels like silk.

Hazel hurries over with tiny pattering steps. "Oh! That is mine," she whispers. Then she stops, holds it to her chest, and lets out a long breath. The fire crackles.

"I am writing a book," she says. "A real one. I have never told anybody." {name} gasps so dramatically it has to lie down on the rug. Hazel giggles, a sound like a teaspoon on a cup. "You may keep the secret with me," she says. "It is much nicer to share it."

title: A Name for the Hero

The fire is going today, and its warmth spreads across your knees like a blanket. Hazel taps her pencil against her spectacles, tick tick tick.

"My story needs a hero," she says. "Small. Round. Brave in a cozy sort of way. But I cannot think of a name."

{name} raises a paw very high. "Sir Fluffington the Magnificent," it suggests. Hazel writes it down politely. "Carrot McNapface," {name} tries next. Hazel writes that down too, with a small snort.

In the end you all agree on something simple and warm. Hazel underlines it twice. The pencil makes a happy little scritch. "Perfect," she whispers. "Now my hero has somewhere to begin."

title: The First Page

Hazel has tucked a wool blanket over the comfy chair, thick and a little scratchy in the coziest way. She sits on the footstool with her notebook open. Her prickles are completely relaxed.

"Just the first page," she says. "Tell me honestly."

Her quiet voice reads about a burrow at the bottom of a hill, where the floor is warm and the door squeaks a friendly squeak. {name} listens with both ears straight up. When she finishes, the only sound is the fire, popping softly.

"That is my burrow," {name} whispers. "My door does squeak like that." Hazel smiles behind her notebook. "Writers borrow from friends," she says. "I hope you do not mind." {name} does not mind one bit.

title: Pip Almost Finds Out

Something fluffy whooshes past your arm, and then fast little feet skid across the library floor. "Hello hello hello," says Pip. "Is anyone writing anything newsworthy today?"

Hazel freezes. The notebook is right there on the table. Very calmly, {name} sits on it. The leather cover is cool and lumpy under its tail.

Hazel lets out the tiniest laugh. "You sat on my book," she whispers. "I am a very good bookmark," says {name}, and does not get up for some time, because it is comfortable.

title: A Cover from Bramble

There is a new smell in the library this week, warm wood and fresh glue. Hazel guides your hands to a book cover on the table. It is smooth board, with a soft cloth spine and little bumpy letters pressed into the front.

"Bramble made it," Hazel whispers. "He did not ask what it was for. He only hummed." Somewhere outside, you can still hear Bramble's toolbox clanking down the lane, and a cheerful hum fading with it.

{name} runs its paw over the letters again and again. "It is a real book now," it says. "It has a jacket. Books with jackets are very serious." Hazel adjusts her spectacles proudly. "Almost finished," she says. "Just the ending left."

title: Writing by the Fire

The fire is extra cozy today, and the room is so quiet you can hear the clock tick, slow and steady. Hazel sits with her notebook on her knees. Her pencil goes scritch, pause, scritch, pause.

{name} keeps watch from the comfy chair. Its job is very important: to say "you can do it" every few minutes, and to pass the biscuits. The biscuits are crumbly and buttery. {name} also tests a few, for safety.

Hazel does not mind. She writes, and thinks, and writes some more. When she looks up her spectacles are crooked and her prickles are puffed with happiness. "I know how it ends now," she whispers. "Next week, I will read it to you."

title: The Last Line

This week Hazel greets you at the door herself, and her paws are trembling a little with excitement, like a kettle just before it sings.

"I wrote the last line this morning," she whispers. "Right as Rocco crowed. It made me jump, and then it made me laugh, and then I knew exactly what to write."

She does not read it yet. She just holds the finished book against your hands so you can feel how thick it is, every page full. It is heavier than you expect, and warm from her paws.

{name} hops in a circle three times, which is the official bunny dance for big news. Hazel lights the fire for tomorrow. "Bring a blanket," she says. "And bring yourself. That is the important part."
